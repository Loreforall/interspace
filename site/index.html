02 / MATHEMATICAL MODEL

From physical laws to discretized equations

01 — GEOMETRY
X(θ, φ) = R [sinφ cosθ, sinφ sinθ, cosφ]

A hemispherical structural mesh, with its equator at z = 0. Discrete elements approximate a material-supported surface.

02 — ELASTIC TRANSPORT
ρ ü = ∇ · σ + b

Continuum momentum balance motivates stress-wave transport. The discrete network requires effective-property calibration.

03 — ACTUATOR POWER
Pₐ꜀ₜ = τₐ꜀ₜ q̇

Deployment requires finite mechanical work and force. Tested configurations exceeded illustrative actuator limits.

04 — ENERGY RECOVERY
dE/dt = F ẋ − c ẋ² − V²/R

Reciprocal piezoelectric coupling transfers energy between mechanical and electrical states. Load dissipation is not stored energy.

03 / ENGINEERING EVIDENCE

Verification, feasibility & known boundaries

NUMERICAL70 automated tests

Stage 12 package reports 70 passing tests. They check code behavior and selected physical consistency conditions.

CONTACTTriangular surface model

Closest-point and barycentric force distribution, using frictionless penalty contact; not full continuum contact.

CONSTRAINTDeployment not demonstrated feasible

None of four prescribed deployment cases met the illustrative power and force limits.

CALIBRATIONExperimental calibration outstanding

No verified material, actuator, rectifier or physical prototype. A mathematical model is not evidence of buildability.
