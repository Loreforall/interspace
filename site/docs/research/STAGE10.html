# Stage 10 — Off-axis transport and ring harvesting (initial integrated build)

Run `python -m examples.run_transport3d` and `pytest -q` from the project root.

## Implemented
- Nearest-node arbitrary polar/azimuthal impact location on an already deployed 3D shell; projectile still moves vertically and contacts a single node.
- Mechanical disturbance propagates through the shell's mass–spring network to the fixed support ring.
- Signed integrated mechanical power flux through spring/damper links adjoining the ring (net flux, **not** recoverable energy).
- Reciprocal piezoelectric transducer dynamics on ring-adjacent links: force `-theta*V` and `C*dV/dt = theta*v_rel - V/R`.
- Separate electrical capacitor energy, resistor dissipation, structural heat, support-damper heat, and total energy residual.
- Centered/off-axis/no-piezo experiments and five additional tests.

## Critical limits and incomplete Stage 10 acceptance items
- Electrical energy is **dissipated in a resistor**, not recovered into a storage circuit. No battery/supercapacitor or saturation feedback yet.
- The support ring is perfectly fixed, and support mechanical flux is not an independent battery-energy quantity.
- The shell is a reduced-order network with uncalibrated parameters, not an atomistic or experimentally validated continuum material.
- No simultaneous deployment/impact coupling: Stage 9's prescribed deployment exceeded the example power and force limits and remains unresolved.
- Off-axis contact uses the nearest surface node and vertical force only, not full sphere–surface contact with friction or tangential momentum.
- Propagation timing needs dedicated wavefront / sensor measurements and mesh/timestep convergence; energy residual alone does not prove these.
- Electric conversion is tiny for illustrative parameters; no performance advantage is claimed.
